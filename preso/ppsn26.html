⚰ Moore's law is dead

Clock speed is not improving any more, many architectural improvements made performance increase in the last few years, but we are already in the diminishing returns territory: lots of architecturar improvements are needed to extract a tiny bit of performance improvement. Besides, Moore's said that power consumption and speed could be improved simultaneously. That's no longer true. By the way, there's a connection between both: a bourgeois middle class needs a new solution to its spiritual needs, hence Geert Groote and its devotio moderna. We are plotting the PKG here, that is, what is consumed by the system-on-chip, which is normally the most important part of energy consumption. What we see is that, for every file, there is roughly a proportion between time and energy spent; however, this proportion is different for every one, indicating that the way energy is tapped is different. Also, there's a certain, mostly horizontal, spread (that is, you can consume a range of energy even spending the same time). Spread can be 1-2 joules in some cases, around 10% of the total measure. What we see here is that there's pretty much a linear increase between the size of the file, time needed to process it and energy spent. Baseline sandwich ⇒ a workload between 2 baseline runs

One of the main concepts in futurism was "simultaneity", like this drawing by Depero called "Simultaneità giroplastiche (trottole)"; at the same time your program is running and the system is trying to control the operating conditions, so the conditions you have before are going to be different to what you will see after; like these spinning tops that simultaneously seem to change the configuration of the space itself. Any program will change the temperature of the processor so that the operating conditions for the next experiment will change. If we only use the one before, we might be missing part of the context
